Recent advances in machine learning have prompted renewed interest in the reliability of automated text classification systems. This study investigates the extent to which feature-weighted heuristics can approximate the performance of supervised classifiers when applied to mixed-authorship documents. We evaluate a transparent scoring framework across three corpora and observe that, while aggregate accuracy remains moderate, the framework provides interpretable per-sentence signals that are useful for revision rather than adjudication. Our findings suggest that calibration against domain-specific reference material substantially improves separation between human-authored and machine-generated passages. Furthermore, we demonstrate that confidence estimates derived from feature dispersion correlate with observed error rates, which supports the use of explicit uncertainty reporting. We conclude that transparent, locally executed detectors occupy a distinct and complementary role relative to commercial black-box services.
